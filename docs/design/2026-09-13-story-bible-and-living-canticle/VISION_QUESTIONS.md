# Five founder questions — Firstlight's total vision

Requested by Dom after Draft B and The Living Canticle Draft C. These are open design questions, not assumed answers or permission to implement the examples. Answer in scenes and ordinary language; no technical specification is needed.

## 1. What does your perfect ordinary evening in Firstlight look like?

You already know the controls and have two uninterrupted hours. Walk through what you actually do from logging in to logging out: where you go, who is with you, what you pursue, and what makes you reluctant to leave. Focus on the experience you would return for regularly, not just the biggest cinematic in the campaign.

## 2. How much freedom should a player have to become someone the prophecy did not expect?

Could someone refuse the heroic path and build a satisfying life as a merchant, explorer, ruler, or musician? Could they genuinely serve Hell, betray an ally, and later seek redemption? Describe the furthest meaningful departure from the intended hero that you want Firstlight to support.

## 3. What should remain permanently lost, even in a world with resurrection?

Briar's return is part of your vision. Beyond that, decide where hope meets consequence: a beloved NPC, a relationship, a ruined town, a companion's trust, or the results of the player's own decisions. What loss would make the story more powerful, and what would feel like the game betrayed the player's investment?

## 4. What kind of society should players be able to create together?

Picture a server a year after launch. Describe the player-made story you most want to hear: enduring friendships, a famous guild, a thriving town, a merchant dynasty, a rivalry, an alliance, or a war. Include the boundary on what another player should be able to change or take from someone's life in the game.

## 5. What do you want Firstlight to leave inside someone after they stop playing?

Beyond beautiful places and enjoyable mechanics, what should they carry back into real life about love, grief, faith, freedom, power, or belonging? State it in your own words, including any message you would never want the game to imply. This is the meaning we should preserve when story and gameplay decisions conflict.
